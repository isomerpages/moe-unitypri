<h5><strong>Teaching and Learning</strong></h5>
<p>Unity Primary School English Language Curriculum is closely guided by<strong> STELLAR</strong> (<strong>S</strong>Trategies
for <strong>E</strong>nglish <strong>L</strong>anguage <strong>L</strong>earning <strong>A</strong>nd <strong>R</strong>eading)
– Singapore’s MOE-developed approach to teaching English in primary schools.
It is an English Language instructional programme that provides current
and relevant instructional materials, teaching strategies and training
to enable teachers to build strong language skills and a love for reading.</p>
<p>STELLAR focuses centrally on helping students to make connections to what
and how they learn:</p>
<p>·&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Text-to-Self Connection – students
are guided to relate what they read to their own experiences,</p>
<p>·&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Text-to-text Connection - students
connect to the text to other books, stories, videos or texts they have
read or seen,</p>
<p>·&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Text-to-World Connection – students
link the text to real-world issues, events or general knowledge</p>
<p>Specifically in UPS, students are guided in the use of <strong>SEES</strong> Strategy
for Simulated-based Conversation tasks. This helps them to make the content
of the conversation richer.</p>

<h5><strong>Key Programmes</strong></h5>
<p><strong><u>Library Programme</u></strong>
</p>
<p>With the newly revamped library, the school seeks to encourage a positive
attitude towards books and support students’ learning by offering a rich
range of resources. We create an environment that strengthens students’
academic, intellectual and personal development.</p>
<p>To inspire a genuine love for reading and continuous learning, the school
partners various departments and organisations such as the National Library
Board to organise reading programmes, book talks and provide inviting spaces
where students can read, work together and explore new ideas.</p>

<p><strong> <u>Class Library Visits</u></strong>
</p>
<p>Helping students discover the pleasure of reading is a consistent part
of the school’s learning experience. Every week, one English lesson is
devoted to the reading programme, where students take part in activities
such as stories shared by teachers and book spotlights. On alternate weeks,
classes head to the library to borrow books and engage in simple reading-related
tasks. The library also works with various departments during events like
Language Festival and Save the Earth event by highlighting books and news

<h4><strong>Language Festival</strong></h4>
<p>The annual Language Festival is a signature programme designed to develop
critical thinkers and confident communicators. At the event, students are
treated to a wide range of engaging and interactive language-based games
and activities. Students immerse themselves in thoughtfully designed activities
that stretch their linguistic skills, broaden their vocabulary, and deepen
their understanding of language concepts in a fun and meaningful way. Through
“Excite, Engage, Express!” assembly programme, they are given opportunities
to showcase their creativity and public speaking skills. There are also
Author’s Talk and Author’s Forum where many students go into deep engagement
and meaningful discussions with the author.</p>
<p></p>
<div class="isomer-image-wrapper">
<img style="width: 100%" height="auto" width="100%" alt="" src="/images/Curriculum/2026/2.png">
</div>
<p>Back in their classrooms, students take part in a range of engaging language
activities, including Bingo, Charade, Jeopardy, Blind Artist, card games,
board games and word games. These fun-filled sessions make English learning
enjoyable while enriching their understanding and appreciation of the language.&nbsp;
At the end of the event, students experience Storigami guided by teachers
telling stories using origami and participate in the Charity Book Drive,
Read for Books, organised by National Library Board (NLB).</p>

<h4><strong>UPSGEM</strong></h4>
<p>Unity Primary Speaks Good English Movement (UPSGEM) capitalises on the
pre-assembly periods to teach students the differences between Singlish/improper
English Language use and Standard English Language. Students are encouraged
to pronounce and spell commonly-mispronounced words and develop an awareness
of proper English in everyday situations.</p>

<h4><strong>Digital Puppetry</strong></h4>
<p>To better engage students in reading and speaking, the department introduced
Moo-O to P1 to P3 students. Moo-O is a digital puppetry software that is
fun and engaging to help students improve their oracy skills. It is designed
to motivate students to read fluently and expressively and promote self-directed
learning.&nbsp;</p>

<h4><strong><u>kidsREAD Programme</u></strong></h4>
<p>Since its inception in 2022, kidsREAD aims to promote the love of reading
and cultivate good reading habits amongst children. The programme leverages
the strengths of various partners to create a reading programme ustomized
to meet the reading needs of selected children between ages 4 and 8. UPS
collaborates with Unity Secondary School to conduct meaningful reading
sessions for these students.</p>
<h4><strong><u>Learning Support Programme (LSP)</u></strong></h4>
<p>The LSP is&nbsp;an early intervention programme aimed at building literacy
skills for Primary 1 and Primary 2 students.&nbsp; This programme is to
provide targeted learning support for students in areas such as reading
and spelling.</p>
<h4><strong><u>School-based Dyslexia Remediation (SDR)</u></strong></h4>
<p>The SDR programme is a specialized literacy remediation programme for
Primary 3 and Primary 4 students with dyslexia.&nbsp; It aims to enable
students to become strategic decoders and spellers whereby different strategies
for reading, spelling and comprehension are taught.</p>
<h4><strong><u>Reading Remediation Programme (RRP)</u></strong></h4>
<p>The RRP is an additional literacy support programme implemented at Primary
3 and Primary 4 to support students with reading difficulties but who do
not have dyslexia.&nbsp;</p>
